Art that wakes up when you point a phone at it.

Students finish a piece, animate it, and hang it up. Anyone with a phone scans one code and every artwork in the room comes alive. No app, no account.

Open the demo gallery

Try it in two minutes

  1. On a laptop, click one of the three demo pieces on this page to open it full size. Or print it.
  2. On a phone or iPad, scan the code below (or open the demo gallery) and tap Start the camera.
  3. Point the camera at the artwork from about an arm's length. The same code works for all three.
  4. Scan with your phone's camera. No app to install.

How a class gallery comes together

Four steps. Students do the first two. The teacher does the rest in about ten minutes.

Make
Make

Students finish a piece, photograph it on the student page, and find out in a few seconds whether a camera can recognize it. Then they animate on top of that photo, so the piece seems to wake up rather than get covered by a video.

Hand in
Hand in

Each student tries the piece with their own camera, types a title and a statement, and hands in one file. Schoology, OneDrive or a shared folder all work. The student handout walks them through it.

Build
Build

Drop everything into the Gallery Builder. It sorts the files, fixes oversized and wrong-format videos, erases location data, lets you try the gallery, and packs one zip plus printable labels.

Show
Show

The Digital Learning Coordinator publishes the zip. Print the poster and labels and hang them beside the work. Each label also opens that student's own piece, so families can share it.

Artwork that tracks well

  • Bold, different shapes all over the page. Outlines, texture, lettering, a detailed background.
  • High contrast. Darks next to lights.
  • Nothing that repeats. Grids, tiles, stripes and rows of identical shapes look the same to a camera everywhere.
  • No big plain areas. A few flat shapes on a blank page give the camera nothing to hold.
  • Matte, flat and well lit. Glare and curled paper hide detail.
  • Photo cropped to the edges of the artwork, taken straight on.

Videos that look right

  • First frame = the finished artwork. This makes the piece come alive instead of a video popping up.
  • Same shape as the artwork photo.
  • MP4, 5 to 15 seconds. The student page and the builder shrink large videos and convert iPhone HEVC video, which Chromebooks can't play.
  • Sound is optional. Viewers tap the speaker to hear it.

Nothing is collected

Living Art runs in the browser. The camera image never leaves the viewer's phone. There are no accounts, no analytics and no uploads from viewers. What goes online is the artwork, its video, a title and a first name with last initial. The student page and the builder erase file names, photo location data and camera details before anything is published.
